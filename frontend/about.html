Keeping local knowledge alive

Skills worth sharing, traditions worth preserving

LifeTrace brings craftspeople and learners together to record and practise Uganda's traditional crafts through clear instructions, photographs and demonstrations.

What is LifeTrace?

LifeTrace is a learning and knowledge-sharing space for local practical skills. Craftspeople can document how they work, including local names, materials, tools and making stages. Learners can explore those guides, practise at their own pace and share their work for feedback.

Why preserve traditional crafts?

Many skills are taught through watching and practising alongside an experienced craftsperson. When there are fewer opportunities to learn together, valuable techniques and terminology can be lost. Thoughtful documentation helps keep these skills visible and makes it easier for future generations to learn.

How it works

Craftspeople share

Experts record their processes with step-by-step guidance, photographs and demonstrations.

Learners practise

Learners browse craft guides, follow the stages and keep track of their progress.

Knowledge is reviewed

Reviewers can provide feedback on clarity, completeness and safe practice before a guide is shared.

Culture, consent and attribution come first

Traditional knowledge belongs to the people and communities who hold it. LifeTrace does not claim ownership of that knowledge. Contributors choose what they are comfortable sharing, and their names and community attribution are respected according to their preferences.
