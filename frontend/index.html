Cultural Heritage Mandate

Why Preserving Local Knowledge Matters

Traditional crafts represent centuries of ecological harmony, botanical mastery, and mathematical ingenuity. When elder craftspeople retire without digital records, irreplaceable human heritage disappears forever.

Intergenerational Transfer

Connect younger digital-native learners directly with master artisans, bridging the knowledge gap through video walkthroughs and photographic stage records.

Sustainable Organic Materials

Preserve botanical knowledge of banana pseudostem fibers (Ebyai), wild date palm fronds (Enkindu), natural hibiscus dyes, and anthill clays.

Attribution & Cultural Respect

Encourage clear attribution and respect for community guidance when traditional knowledge is documented and shared.

Demonstrated Practical Mastery

Learners don't just watch videos—they make the products, upload photographic proof of their work, receive expert grading, and earn authentic certificates.

The Preservation Workflow

How LifeTrace Protects Local Skills

A community-led process that helps preserve local knowledge and support practical learning.

1. Expert Documents

Craftspeople record local names, material harvesting, tools, step-by-step photos, safety warnings, and video demonstrations.

2. Reviewer Verifies

Standards reviewers scrutinize clarity, step integrity, safety compliance, and community attribution before approving publication.

3. Learner Practises

Learners follow procedures, mark step completions, produce their handmade craft, and upload photographic evidence of their work.

4. Verified Certification

Learners can earn certificates after completing the required practical work and assessments.
